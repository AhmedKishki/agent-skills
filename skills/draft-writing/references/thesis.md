# Thesis

## Purpose

Record what the article argues, investigates and seeks to show in `{project}-thesis.md`.

## Contains

- The central thesis or governing inquiry.
- The article's purpose.
- Main questions.
- Article-wide interpretations and metatheoretical commitments.
- Political commitments that define the argument.

## Excludes

- Writing and publication requirements.
- Source excerpts.
- Unordered component arguments.
- Article order or passage plans.
- Draft prose.

## Procedure

1. Separate thesis material from draft direction and reusable wording through [Input routing](input-routing.md).
2. Present a substantive thesis addition or change exactly and obtain approval.
3. Keep approved commitments operative until the user replaces the affected aspect.
4. Preserve replaced text under `## Superseded thesis material` with its replacement.
5. Identify affected arguments, synthesis, article-arc units and draft prose.

## Format

```markdown
# Thesis

> **Editing note:** You may edit this file directly. The assistant will preserve replaced approved material and report affected downstream work.

## Central thesis

<What the article argues.>

## Article purpose

<What the inquiry is for.>

## Main questions

- <Question>

## Governing commitments

### <Short commitment heading>

<Approved commitment.>

## Open questions

- None.

## Superseded thesis material

### <Earlier commitment>

**Replaced by:** <current heading or user-material ID>

<Preserved earlier text.>
```

## Completion

The purpose, central thesis and main questions are clear enough to guide research and argument formation, with no unresolved contradiction blocking them.
