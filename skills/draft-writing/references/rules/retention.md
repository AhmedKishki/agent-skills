# Retention

Read the affected owner and its dependants before removing or replacing content. Keep current approved direction, material and evidence needed by active or planned work, exact inputs needed for provenance, unresolved questions, future actions and the minimal section handoff.

After an approved replacement, remove superseded wording, resolved blockers, rejected alternatives and completed process narratives. Do not create archive, unassigned-material, decision-history, general material or global progress files. Do not retain empty fields or repeat rules in each owner.

A passage already inserted in a draft may still be a live source of wording or provenance. Do not delete approved material just to shorten a file. Identify what is superseded or no longer needed, explain affected references, and obtain the required removal approval under [decisions](decisions.md).

Before removing unique approved wording, verify that it is recoverable. Git is not a backup for uncommitted user changes. If the history is absent or uncertain, preserve the wording and ask; never discard it on the assumption that someone committed it.

Keep provenance in a current exact owner or an explicit Git locator naming repository, full commit, file and item/span. Verify that the locator recovers the exact input before replacing a live reference with it. Do not leave broken basis links or make future synthesis depend on remembered chat text. Historical AI authorship and cross-section exclusions still apply to recovered content.

For example, after replacing an approved paragraph, remove its superseded live version only after checking recoverability and affected bases. Remove the resolved amendment task from progress but keep its next ID counter unchanged.

At session end, leave only the current object and approval state, live blockers, exact next action and necessary counters in local progress. Remove settled proposal text and completed construction logs. Keep unresolved article-level questions beside their article owner; the article plan is not a second handoff.

The skill's release `changelog.md` is the release-history exception. Keep it outside routine startup, drafting, synthesis, smoothing and handoff reads. It records past releases, not current rules. Do not create an article changelog to imitate it.
