# Formulaic phrases

These phrases often mark a sentence with no connection. Treat each one as a prompt to check that sentence's justification. Keep the phrase when it is precise and does work in context. Clusters tell you more than single words.

- generic significance: "pivotal moment", "broader landscape", "stands as a testament"
- superficial participles: "highlighting", "underscoring", "showcasing", "reflecting"
- vague attribution: "experts say", "studies suggest", "critics argue"
- promotional words: "vibrant", "rich heritage", "breathtaking", "renowned"
- canned balance: "while X, it is important to recognise Y"
- negative parallelism: "not merely X but Y", "it's not just X, it's Y" (test against rule 5)
- false comprehensiveness: forced threes, sweeping "from X to Y"
- aphorism templates: "X is the language of Y", "X becomes a trap"
- inflated verbs for simple relations: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts"
- synonym cycling: renaming one thing to avoid repeating a word. Repeat the term when the meaning stays the same.
- generic openings and closings: "in today's world", "it is worth noting", "the future remains bright"
- assistant residue: "certainly", "great question", "I hope this helps", "let me know if you would like"
- uniform shape: paragraphs of equal length, a summary sentence after each paragraph, repeated transition formulas
- manufactured punch: runs of one-line paragraphs or short sentences that each try to land as a verdict
