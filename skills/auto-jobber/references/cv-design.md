# Design a CV around the applicant

## Keep each layer independent

Retain facts separately from professional positioning and document presentation. New qualifications and factual corrections change the retained data after source review. Profile wording develops with the applicant's goals and own input. Selection, emphasis, section order and appearance may change for an opportunity without changing the underlying facts.

Treat an imported CV as source material, not a compulsory layout or a list that every later CV must reproduce. Keep omitted qualifications available in the retained data. Optional projects, interests and secondary tools belong in a CV when they strengthen its purpose, not merely because they exist.

## Establish the information hierarchy

Agree the template, information order, emphasis and page target with the applicant, or make a reasoned proposal for their review. These are design decisions, not fixed skill policy. Included templates and source code are reusable starting points that may be adapted or replaced.

Start from the role or the applicant's stated direction. Make the professional identity, a concise factual introduction and the strongest relevant skills easy to find. Give recent, relevant work more weight than older or less relevant material; use a research-led order when the opportunity calls for it.

Show the person through their actual work, projects, interests and applicant-supplied working style. Do not infer personality from a photograph or qualifications, and do not add generic claims such as being passionate, exceptional or a team player without the applicant's input. Proposed profile prose remains a draft until reviewed.

Within work and education, make the relationship between role or degree, organisation, date range and place evident. Use a consistent date/location rail or another clear grouping. Preserve month-versus-year precision. Do not invent dates or geographical locations for projects, certificates or publications.

Remove redundant wording before reducing the font size. State core tools once in a skills overview; repeat them in an experience description only when doing so adds useful evidence of their application. Prefer specific responsibilities and research topics to repeated lists of programming languages.

## Use the page deliberately

Choose length from the useful material and the intended reader. Prefer a compact, readable page when it fits; add a page when real content needs it. Do not force a two-page split that leaves large unused areas, add filler to occupy space, or shrink text until it is difficult to read.

Use hierarchy, alignment, balanced columns and restrained spacing to distinguish sections. Dates and places should be clear without competing with the role or skill. Avoid decorative charts, unsupported proficiency scores and large empty sidebars. A portrait is optional and must be the applicant's supplied or authorized image, not a substitute or generated person.

Keep the editable HTML/CSS portable, with system fonts and supported local assets. Preserve actual documents and their selected sources rather than storing multiple competing masters.

Deliver design revisions under SKILL's PDF default and Tailoring's rendering checks. Keep entry-linked comments available under Entry notes without treating their existence as a reason to add them to the CV.

## Review a design revision

Changing section selection, ordering, a portrait or layout is a normal reviewed source revision. Prepare the new source, audit it against the same retained facts, preview it in each needed language and obtain approval before sealing. Keep earlier baselines and application snapshots intact.

When the native snapshot helpers are used, tailoring budgets apply after the chosen baseline has been captured for an application. They do not require every application to reuse an unsuitable visual design; select and review the appropriate baseline before creating its snapshot. Do not silently bypass the budgets within an existing snapshot.

Inspect every rendered page for clipping, overflow, broken date/location associations, awkward column gaps, unused space, readable body type and the actual portrait crop. Check that the most relevant information is encountered first and that translations preserve the same qualification history. Rendering and integrity checks do not establish factual truth or applicant approval.
