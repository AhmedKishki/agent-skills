# Draft the document together, by questionnaire

## Aim

Work with the applicant practically: answer the questions the document must answer, and do not overwhelm them with information. The applicant owns the facts, motivation and wording; the agent asks, suggests and assembles. This process applies to cover and motivation letters, research statements and form answers; Voice and Research statement still set what the finished document must achieve.

## Steps

1. Present the employer's or group's topics, taken from the offer and its public work (see [Research](research.md)), with a short assessment of how each fits the applicant's records. Label the assessment as the agent's. The applicant chooses the topics the document centres on.
2. Agree with the applicant the few questions the document must answer, taken from the offer. For a PhD statement these may be: which topics excite you, where you would take them, what you bring, and why a doctorate in this group.
3. Ask them as a short questionnaire, one document question at a time. Each question names the document question it serves and where the answer goes in the document. Offer suggestions as options and label each one as the agent's (AI); the applicant can always answer in their own words. Record the answers verbatim with their source in the application records, not in the conversation.
4. Keep the employer's direction, in the employer's own words, as the document's main context. Anything else the applicant shares shows skill, experience, motivation or interest.
5. Write the first draft as one plain Markdown file in the application records. It holds only the document's text, with open points in [brackets] where they belong and agent wording in italics. Keep provenance, notes and decisions in the other records.
6. Close the open points with further questions. Then go through the italic passages and a final polish together, offering each change as an option to pick. The applicant may edit the file directly: re-read it before changing it and keep their edits.
7. Form the final document in its delivery shape, such as a letter with sender, recipient, date, subject, salutation and closing.
8. Render the PDF only once every open point is closed, then apply [Tailoring](tailoring.md)'s rendering and every-page checks. When the offer asks for an emailed application, draft a short covering email. If the applicant assembles the files into one package, check it page by page against the materials the offer requires.

## Research questions and links

- Never turn the applicant's work or projects into proposed research questions; they show background only.
- If the applicant has no research question yet, the document names the employer's open problems they want to work on, in the employer's words, and may say that the applicant wishes to find their question within them.
- State a link between the applicant's experience and the employer's problem only where the applicant has confirmed that particular link. Otherwise present the background in time order and leave the connection to the reader.

## Letter order

Unless the offer's requests need another order, a motivation letter or letter-shaped statement runs:

1. The application and the topics that excite the applicant, in the opening paragraph.
2. The evidence of fit: the applicant's experience and skills, each item broken down.
3. The motivation and interest in this employer, such as a project the applicant would like to join.
4. The personal part, such as what first drew them to the field, last.

## Keep it light

- Keep replies short: what changed, in a line or two, then the next question.
- Do not produce HTML, PDF or extra files for working drafts unless the applicant asks.
- Raise only the open points the applicant can act on, and say why each one matters.
